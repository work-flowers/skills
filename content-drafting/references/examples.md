# Content Examples

Annotated samples showing the work.flowers writing style in action.

## Blog Post Example: "How to Never Lose a Meeting Action Item Again"

**Opening (problem-first hook):**
> Every founder I know has the same problem: you leave a meeting with a head full of action items, and half of them evaporate before you've finished your coffee.

Note: Starts with a relatable pain point. "Every founder I know" establishes credibility without being self-important. The coffee detail makes it concrete.

**Personal context:**
> I've tried every system. Handwritten notes. Voice memos. Manually logging items in a task tracker. The optimistic, "I'll just remember it." None of them stuck.

Note: Short, punchy list in natural prose (not bullets). Shows the author has skin in the game.

**Clear problem statement:**
> The gap between "documented" and "actionable" is where things go to die.

Note: Memorable one-liner that crystallises the insight.

**Technical explanation with accessibility:**
> In plain language, while overall employment growth for software developers and other AI-exposed jobs look stable, the youngest workers in these roles are losing ground - and the timing lines up with the launch of ChatGPT in late 2022.

Note: "In plain language" signals a shift from technical detail to accessible summary.

**Closing CTA (soft sell):**
> If you want help setting this up, or building something similar for your team, [let's talk]({{https://calendar.notion.so/meet/dennis-0l10rj1xnv/kb58d4lwv}}).

Note: Invitation, not pressure. Links to calendar directly.

## Social Media Example: "Ads Are Coming to ChatGPT"

**Hook:**
> After dancing around the subject for years, OpenAI has finally announced that they're rolling out ads to free ChatGPT users in the next few weeks.

Note: "After dancing around the subject for years" adds context and mild editorial voice without being preachy.

**Business context with perspective:**
> From a business perspective, this makes complete sense and is probably long overdue. (Their failure to launch an ads business sooner has been driving Ben Thompson at Stratechery nuts.)

Note: Parenthetical adds personality and signals the author reads widely. Shows fair-mindedness before critique.

**Pivot to skepticism:**
> For now, OpenAI is saying all the right things about their approach to ads... HOWEVER, OpenAI has a long track record of saying one thing and doing another.

Note: Acknowledges the official position, then pivots with "HOWEVER" (capitalised for emphasis). Direct without being unfair.

**Concrete evidence:**
> We saw this with the GPT-4o "sycophancy" correction, where safety adjustments were reportedly rolled back after they led to a drop in user engagement.

Note: Specific example, not vague accusation.

**Closing with honest take:**
> So, to recap: advertising is exactly the right move for OpenAI from a strategic and commercial perspective. But I remain highly skeptical of their claims to implement ads in a way that prioritizes privacy, safety, and user experience. Hope I'm wrong, but I know I'm not.

Note: "Hope I'm wrong, but I know I'm not" — memorable closing that's confident without being arrogant.

## Social Media Example: "Cover Letters and AI"

**Hook with data:**
> Before ChatGPT arrived, freelancers who wrote stellar cover letters earned an extra $26 per task, a huge bonus on platforms where the median gig pays $100.

Note: Specific numbers ($26, $100) make it concrete and memorable.

**Consequence:**
> Then, with the advent of generative AI, everyone could suddenly craft lengthy, "personalized" applications. Median cover letter length jumped from 79 words to 104 words.

Note: More specific numbers. Scare quotes around "personalized" do editorial work without lengthy explanation.

**Punchline:**
> Predictably, employers completely stopped caring, as the signaling value of a lengthy cover letter evaporated, along with that $26 premium.

Note: "Predictably" signals this was obvious in hindsight. Clean cause-and-effect.

**Broader observation:**
> The entire hiring process has become an AI slop-powered nuclear arms race that's actually a race to the bottom, and we are consequently becoming more reliant on subjective factors rather than less.

Note: "AI slop-powered nuclear arms race" — vivid metaphor that captures the absurdity.

## Patterns to Replicate

1. **Numbers beat adjectives**: "$26 premium" > "significant bonus"
2. **Name sources**: "Ben Thompson at Stratechery" builds credibility
3. **Editorial voice through word choice**: "dancing around", "predictably", "slop-powered"
4. **Acknowledge complexity before taking a position**: "This makes complete sense... HOWEVER"
5. **End with a question or memorable line**, not a generic CTA
6. **Tag companies on LinkedIn**: Use native @ mentions when posting (not markdown links)

## Patterns to Avoid

1. ❌ "This isn't just about cover letters—it's about the future of hiring"
2. ❌ "In today's rapidly evolving landscape..."
3. ❌ "Let's unpack this"
4. ❌ "Here's why that matters"
5. ❌ Bullet-pointed lists in social posts
6. ❌ Generic conclusions: "What do you think? Let me know in the comments!"
